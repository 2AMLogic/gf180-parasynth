`n_clip` is 0 on all five -- so `bus` and `post` agree to 0.02 dB and the

  WHAT SEPARATES THEM, and it is not the DC reading: both placements
  remove the same sub-20 Hz energy to a hundredth of a dB, at every stack.
  **A blocker in the wrong place still reduces the DC number** -- s2's
  warning, measured rather than repeated. What separates them is the rail:
  removing the standing offset BEFORE the clamp buys back the headroom the
  offset was consuming, so `bus` rails on fewer samples and lands closer to
  the unclamped ideal. `post` filters a waveform the clamp has already
  flattened and cannot recover it. `bus` is the placement that ships.

  THE REFERENCE IS BIASED TOWARDS `post`, AND THAT IS STATED RATHER THAN
  HIDDEN. `ideal` = dc_block(unclamped accumulator) is exactly what `post`
  computes when nothing rails, so `post` reads -600 dBFS (bit-identical)
  on every stack that does not clip, while `bus` differs by about half an
  LSB (-96 dBFS) purely from the order of the integer rounding. The
  conclusion survives a reference that favours the loser: once the rail
  fires, `bus` is 1.21 dB closer to the ideal and rails 18 samples against
  25. A reference built the other way round would only widen that.
